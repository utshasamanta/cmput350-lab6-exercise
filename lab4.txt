Answer written questions here:

1. Starting with a low iteration count makes the first frames render quickly, so the viewer stays
responsive while the user moves or zooms. Higher iteration counts are used after the view stops
changing to improve detail.

2. Divide the image into groups of pixels and give each group to a different thread, core, or
computer. The main computer can wait for the groups to finish and then combine them into the final
image.

3. Sampling four subpixels and averaging their colours would smooth jagged edges and reduce
speckled noise. It would also take about four times as many Mandelbrot calculations per pixel, so
rendering would be slower.
